Understanding Cryptocurrency Fraud

Recognizing the circumstances surrounding a loss can help determine what information and action may be appropriate.

Fraudulent Investment Platforms

Some fraudulent platforms display fabricated balances or returns and may prevent withdrawals or introduce additional demands for payment.

Phishing & Account Compromise

Fraudsters may use deceptive websites, messages, emails, or links to obtain sensitive information or gain unauthorized access.

Impersonation

Criminals may impersonate companies, support representatives, professionals, investigators, or other trusted individuals.

Social Engineering

Fraud can involve the deliberate creation of trust before a victim is persuaded to transfer cryptocurrency or disclose information.

Protecting Your Digital Assets

Security and preservation of relevant information are important following a suspected cryptocurrency fraud incident.

Preserve Relevant Evidence

Keep transaction IDs, wallet addresses, communications, screenshots, dates, platform details, and other records connected with the incident.

Protect Remaining Assets

Review the security of remaining digital assets and take appropriate steps to reduce the possibility of additional unauthorized activity.

Act Promptly

Timely organization of information can help when communicating with exchanges, professionals, reporting bodies, or other relevant parties.

Use Appropriate Channels

Complex legal, forensic, cybersecurity, or investigative matters may require involvement from appropriately qualified professionals.
